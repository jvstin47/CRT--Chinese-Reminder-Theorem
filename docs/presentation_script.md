#  CRT LOCK — 2-Speaker Traffic Signal Presentation Script

**Format:** 2-Speaker Dialogue / Conversation  
**Characters:**  
- **Alex (Speaker 1):** The Everyday Driver / Host (Curious, relatable, asks questions)  
- **Sam (Speaker 2):** The Traffic Tech Lead (Explains concepts simply, runs the live demo)  
**Target Duration:** ~3–4 Minutes  

---

##  Scene 1: The Problem (Stuck in Traffic)

**Alex:**  
> *"Sam, be honest with me... why does it feel like whenever I'm in a rush, every single traffic light turns red right in front of me?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"Haha! It’s actually a classic math problem. Traffic signals along a busy street operate on different timer cycles — like 3 minutes, 5 minutes, or 7 minutes. If they aren't synchronized, you get stuck stopping at every block."*

**Alex:**  
> *"So how do city planners fix that? How do you get a 'Green Wave' where a car can drive straight through all green lights?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"That’s where a 2,000-year-old math theorem called the **Chinese Remainder Theorem** comes in. And to prove it, we built this interactive visual app called **CRT Lock**!"*

---

##  Scene 2: The Traffic Signal Analogy

**Sam:** *(Pointing to the interactive web app screen)*  
> *"Imagine our street has 3 traffic signals:*  
> - *Signal 1 changes every **3 minutes**.*  
> - *Signal 2 changes every **5 minutes**.*  
> - *Signal 3 changes every **7 minutes**.*  
> 
> *In our app, each rotating gear is a traffic signal. The slot in the gear represents a **GREEN LIGHT**, and this sliding key rod is your car trying to drive through."*

**Alex:**  
> *"Got it! So if all three gear slots line up, the rod slides through — meaning my car hits green lights across the whole street?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"Spot on! But if even ONE signal is red, the gear blocks the rod — **CLACK!** — and your car is forced to stop."*

---

##  Scene 3: Live Interactive Demo

**Alex:**  
> *"Alright, let's test it! Suppose Signal A turned green 2 minutes ago, Signal B turned green 3 minutes ago, and Signal C turned green 2 minutes ago. What minute should I dial?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"Let's try a random guess first. Give me a number on the dial."*

**Alex:**  
> *"Let's try Minute **20**!"*

**Sam:** *(Sets main dial to 20)*  
> *"Okay! At Minute 20:*  
> - *Signal 1 (3-min timer): GREEN light!*  
> - *Signal 2 (5-min timer): RED light!*  
> - *Signal 3 (7-min timer): RED light!*  
> 
> *Let's hit **Check Lock** to try driving through..."*  
> *(Clicks 'Check Lock' — Loud THUD sound as rod hits a dead stop)*  
> 
> *"BAM! Red light at Intersection #2! Your car is stuck."*

**Alex:**  
> *"Ouch! Okay, so Minute 20 doesn't work. How do we find the exact minute when ALL THREE turn green?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"That's what the Chinese Remainder Theorem calculates for us. For our 3, 5, and 7 minute timers, the exact magic moment is **Minute 23**! Dial it to 23."*

**Alex:** *(Dials to 23)*  
> *"Look! All three signal lights on the dashboard turned GREEN!"*

**Sam:**  
> *"Now let's drive through..."*  
> *(Clicks 'Check Lock' — Smooth sliding sound, spring latch pops open!)*  
> 
> *"The rod glides straight through all three gears! Click **Open**..."*  
> *(Clicks 'Open' — Door swings open with victory chime)*  
> 
> *"The door opens revealing **23** — the exact green wave minute!"*

---

##  Scene 4: Wrap-Up & Everyday Impact

**Alex:**  
> *"That’s awesome! So the Chinese Remainder Theorem basically finds the one secret timing where different repeating cycles align?"*

**Sam:**  
> *"Exactly. And beyond traffic lights, engineers use this exact same math for fiber-optic internet cables, computer chip processing, and cybersecurity encryption."*

**Alex:**  
> *"It turns heavy textbook math into a visual puzzle you can actually see and hear. Thanks everyone!"*
